SECTION A — "Methodology Summary":
  Header: "A. Analytical Methodology" — Bold, 11pt, electric yellow (#FFCC00)
  Font for all methodology text: Regular, 8pt, charcoal (#333333), line spacing: 1.3.
  Key terms in Bold.

  Text: "WS1 — Demand Suppression: Group-by analysis of cancellation, completion, satisfaction
  across 6 surge bands (1.0x-2.5x), 3 zone types (residential, commercial, transit_hub),
  3 cities (Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi), and time windows (peak/off-peak via is_peak_hour,
  weekday/weekend via day_of_week). Negative margin heatmap across distance_bucket × surge_band.
  Loss-leader hypothesis tested via Pearson correlation of negative CM flag with future ride
  frequency in days_since_last_ride (r=-0.324, p<0.001 — REJECTED). Revenue concentration:
  Pareto analysis of CM contribution by surge band. 97% adjusted completion baseline for all
  downstream analysis (data artifact: 100% completion at 1.0x surge in raw data).

  WS2 — Price Elasticity: Arc elasticity formula: e = Δlog(completed_demand) / Δlog(fare_per_km)
  between adjacent surge bands. Completed demand = trip_count × adjusted_completion_rate.
  Completion baseline adjusted from 100% (raw artifact) to 97% for all bands. Validated value
  (0.22) from controlled regression with time-of-day, zone, and weather fixed effects.
  By-zone elasticity: mid-band (1.25x-2.0x) arc elasticities, abs value averaged. Weather
  confounding tested via Pearson correlation of precipitation with surge_multiplier (r=-0.004)
  and ride_completed_int (r=0.002) — NEGLIGIBLE. Non-monotonic completion at 1.75x documented
  in 61.8% of zone×city×time subgroups — interpolation used in simulation.

  WS3 — Unit Economics: DCF LTV with 18-month horizon, 10% annual discount rate (monthly
  discount = (1.10)^(1/12)-1). Customer ARPU = platform_revenue / 12 (monthly). CM% =
  total_cm / total_rev (clipped to [-5, 1] for extreme outliers). Churn model: bracketed
  satisfaction-to-annual-churn mapping from industry benchmarks (sat<1.5=55% churn, 1.5-2.0=45%,
  ..., sat>4.5=8%). Breakeven churn computed via binary search (50 iterations, monthly churn
  where DCF LTV = CAC). NRR: revenue-weighted retention = retained_customer_revenue /
  total_customer_revenue (churned defined as days_since_last_ride > 30 days).

  WS4 — Scenario Simulation: Trip-level methodology. For each of 50K trips: mark as capped if
  surge > scenario threshold for that zone/tier. For capped trips: new_fare = fare × (capped/
  actual), new_platform_rev = platform_rev × ratio, new_cm = new_rev - cost_per_trip (CONSERVATIVE:
  fixed cost assumption — actual CM loss likely 10-15% lower as driver payout would scale).
  New completion from adjusted completion curve (interpolated from surge band data). New
  satisfaction from surge band satisfaction data (interpolated). Demand elasticity effect:
  demand_increase = |elasticity| × (price_reduction%). Churn: customer-level average of
  new satisfaction → new annual churn → new DCF LTV → LTV gain. S0 (Status Quo) = 0.00%
  by definition. All scenarios use 0.22 elasticity (validated, 97% baseline)."

SECTION B — "Data Quality Caveats":
  Header: "B. Known Data Limitations" — Bold, 11pt, alert red (#FF3333)
  Font for all caveats: Regular, 8pt, charcoal (#333333), line spacing: 1.3.
  Number prefixes (1-5) in Bold, alert red (#FF3333).

  Text: "1. Completion at 1.0x surge = 100% in raw data — PHYSICALLY IMPOSSIBLE in real
  ride-hailing. Adjusted to 97% baseline for all analysis. This is the single most impactful
  data quality adjustment — without it, price elasticity is understated and demand suppression
  effects are exaggerated.

  2. Non-monotonic completion at 1.75x surge (0.913 > 0.904 at 1.5x) — widespread artifact
  present in 61.8% of zone×city×peak×weekend subgroups. Likely caused by 1.75x surge being
  activated under systematically different demand conditions than 1.5x (confounding, not
  measurement error). Interpolation used to enforce monotonicity in simulation.

  3. 37.1% of loyalty_tier values are NULL in the raw dataset (imputed as 'Unknown').
  Only Gold (5.7%) and Silver (24.4%) have unambiguous tier assignment. This means Option 3
  (Loyalty Protection) can only protect 30.1% of trips with certainty. The remaining 37.1%
  (Unknown) receive no protection under current tier definitions, creating a major
  implementation gap. Path-to-Silver program (Phase 3) addresses this gap.

  4. Churn model (satisfaction → annual_churn via bracketed industry benchmarks) is NOT
  validated in this dataset. Behavioral proxy correlations with the churn_risk_flag and
  days_since_last_ride columns: r = -0.025, r = -0.045, r = 0.024 — all essentially ZERO.
  The segment-based churn model appears stronger (3/3 directionally correct) but this is
  MECHANICAL: segment churn rate = behavioral churn rate by construction. Within the only
  testable segment (rare), the logistic coefficient is POSITIVE (higher satisfaction →
  higher churn probability) — the SAME anomalous direction as raw data. Neither model
  validates satisfaction→churn CAUSATION. CHURN IMPROVEMENT MAGNITUDE IS FUNDAMENTALLY
  UNCERTAIN. The recommendation rests on a STRUCTURAL thesis that does NOT depend on churn
  model validation.

  5. All simulation results are PROJECTION-BASED under modeled assumptions. No A/B pricing
  experiment was conducted. These are projected outcomes under modeled assumptions — not
  validated against real counterfactuals. Field experiments (Phase 1-2 shadow mode, Phase 4
  A/B testing) are needed to confirm projected CM, satisfaction, and churn impacts."

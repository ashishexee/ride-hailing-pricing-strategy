
ELASTICITY METHODOLOGY
======================

Method: Arc Elasticity of Completion Rate with respect to Fare per km
Formula: e = (Q1-Q2)/(Q1+Q2) / (P1-P2)/(P1+P2)
Where Q = completion rate, P = average fare_per_km

Baseline: 1.0x surge (100% completion rate, baseline fare_per_km)

ASSUMPTIONS:
1. 1.0x surge represents "normal" baseline behavior (no pricing pressure)
2. Completion rate is a valid proxy for demand (captures intensive margin)
3. Surge multiplier is partially exogenous after controlling for zone and time fixed effects
4. Elasticity is locally constant within each surge band
5. Weather effects are separable from price effects (controls applied)

LIMITATIONS:
1. ENDOGENEITY: Surge activates during high demand → OLS/arc estimates biased toward zero (attenuation bias) → our estimates are LOWER BOUNDS on true elasticity
2. INTENSIVE MARGIN ONLY: We only observe trips that were requested. People who saw surge and didn't open the app (extensive margin) are invisible → true demand suppression is higher
3. NO VALID INSTRUMENT: Surge-as-IV is circular (surge IS the price). Zone×hour FE is the best feasible identification with this data.
4. 100% COMPLETION AT 1.0x: This is likely a data artifact that makes the baseline non-comparable to surged trips. Arc elasticity from 100% to 96% may overstate the change.
5. CROSS-SECTIONAL: Cannot establish causation — only conditional correlations
6. SAMPLE SIZE: Some segment×surge cells have <100 observations — results should be interpreted directionally



WEATHER CONFOUNDING ASSESSMENT (Round 5):
Tested precipitation as potential confounder (correlates with both surge activation and completion).
Results: precip-surge correlation = -0.0041, precip-completion correlation = 0.0021.
Both are effectively zero. Weather-stratified elasticity differs by <5% from unadjusted.
VERDICT: Weather confounding is NEGLIGIBLE in this dataset. Unadjusted elasticity (0.22 at 97% baseline)
is reliable. The earlier claim (R4) of '10-15% lower true elasticity' was based on assumption, not data.
This is actually GOOD for the recommendation: it means our conservative elasticity estimate is accurate.
